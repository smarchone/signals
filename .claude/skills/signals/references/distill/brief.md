# Stage 1 · Brief (`brief`)

The must-read: `business.md`, `product.md`, then `model.md`. Follow `common.md` too.

## business.md: why it wins

1. **Problem and alternatives**: what people do today and why it falls short.
2. **Positioning**: one sentence against the nearest alternatives.
3. **Wedge**: how it gets its first users.
4. **Moat**: what compounds with usage. Be honest about what isn't a moat yet.
5. **Risks**: the top three, each with the signal that would show it's real.

## product.md: what it is and how it feels

1. **One-liner**: the product in one sentence.
2. **For whom**: the primary user and their pain, concretely.
3. **Core loop**: the key interaction as numbered steps (a small ascii diagram is welcome).
4. **Feel**: three to five principles for how it should feel to use.
5. **The moment**: the single moment the user feels the payoff.
6. **Non-goals**: what it deliberately is not.
7. **Open questions**: what the meeting left unresolved.

## model.md: the model

The product as **abstractions built from vocabulary, composed into the product**, high level only. It reads
top-down: each term appears in **bold** the moment the story needs it, with the reason it must exist ("we
need a word for an idea that passed the vote, because it behaves differently from a raw one: a **Signal**
is…"). No glossary here: `vocab.md` pins the terms down later. Once a term is introduced, use it exactly.

1. **The shape**: one paragraph. The product as a composition of its three to five core abstractions.
2. **Core abstractions**: a short paragraph each. Introduce the term, say why it must exist, and name the
   terms it is built from (each with a one-line why).
3. **How they compose**: an ascii diagram and a few sentences walking the core loop from `product.md`
   through the abstractions.

Nothing deeper: the human confirms this shape before the design builds on it.
